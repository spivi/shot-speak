# AI-slop tells: camera and light

Read this file when you check composition or light: each tell, why it reads as AI, and the craft fix.

## 1. Camera composition

| The tell | Why it reads as AI | The craft fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Centred, front-facing subject.** The person stands in the middle, square to the camera. | A film crew almost never stands there. The frame looks like a portrait or an advert. | Shoot from the side or a three-quarter position. Put the subject on one third of the frame. Leave space in the direction of the look or the move. |
| **Eyes into the lens.** | The strongest single tell. A look into the lens ends the story and starts a selfie. | Give the eyes a target inside the scene. Write "nobody looks into the lens". For a close-up, use a clean profile and let only the eyes move. |
| **Eye-level medium shot.** Chest-height camera, waist-up framing, for every shot. | The neutral frame has no point of view. Every generator returns it when the height is not stated. | Select the height for a reason. On the floor for scale. At the subject's own eye height for closeness. High for weakness. Overhead for pattern. |
| **Symmetrical hero framing.** A posed figure, dead centre, shoulders square. | A poster, not a moment. It is the answer to the word "epic". | Use depth in place of symmetry: a dark foreground shape, the subject, a far background. Use symmetry only as a clear decision. |
| **180 or 360 degree orbit.** | The move passes the front of the face, flips left and right on screen, and makes the generator invent the unseen half of the place. | Use a partial arc of 90 to 130 degrees on 1 side of the action. Name the start view and the end view. The arc never reaches the front of the face. |
| **A camera position that no crew could use.** The camera hangs in the middle of the action or inside an object. | The viewer feels that nobody could have filmed it. | Put the camera where a person or a tripod can stand: low and next to the action, or far away on a long lens. If neither works, select another composition. |
| **Flat staging.** Everything at the same distance from the lens. | No depth. The frame reads as a backdrop with a figure in front. | Put 1 thing near the lens, the subject in the middle and 1 thing far away. |

## 2. Lighting

| The tell | Why it reads as AI | The craft fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Soft, even, golden glow.** Light with no source and no shadow. | Real light comes from 1 place and leaves a dark side. Sourceless light is the standard generator finish. | Name 1 motivated source and its direction: a low sun, a window, a lamp. Make it hard when the scene allows. |
| **No real shadow.** Every corner of the frame is readable. | Without black there is no shape and no mood. | Let a share of the frame, about one third, fall into true shadow. Use light and shadow as shapes in the composition. |
| **Front light.** The light comes from behind the camera. | Front light flattens faces and removes texture. | Point the camera toward the light. Use backlight, a rim on hair and shoulders, silhouettes, and shadows that fall toward the lens. |
| **Bloom, haze, fog and god rays.** | Generators add haze to hide flat space. The picture turns milky and the blacks go grey. | Keep the air clear and the blacks deep. If rays matter, give them a real cause: dust that the action lifts, in hard backlight. |
| **Glossy, wet floors.** | A mirror floor is a free way to look expensive. It appears whether it rained or not. | Write "matte, worn floor". Use water only when the story has water. |
| **Lens flare everywhere.** | A flare needs a bright source in the frame or at its edge. A flare with no source is decoration. | Allow 1 small natural flare, and only when the source itself is in the frame. |

A reliable recipe when you have no other idea: a dark place, 1 hard low source, the camera facing into the
light, rims and silhouettes, one third of the frame in deep shadow, no glow, no bloom, no fog.
