# Lens guide

Read this file when you select or explain a lens.

Focal lengths are full-frame equivalents. A lens is a feeling first and a field of view second.

## The 3 facts an amateur needs

1. **A wide lens (18 to 24 mm) shows more and exaggerates depth.** Near things look large and far things look small.
   Moves feel fast. Use it close to the subject.
2. **A long lens (85 to 135 mm) shows less and flattens depth.** The background looks close and soft.
   The subject looks watched from far away. Use it from a distance.
3. **The camera distance changes the perspective. The lens only crops it.** To change how a face looks,
   move the camera, then select the lens that gives the framing.

## Menu

| Lens | What the viewer sees | Use it for | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18 mm | Very large space, big foreground, strong lines | Scale, a camera on the floor, a small jump made heroic | Bends faces and bodies near the frame edge. Keep it level. |
| 24 mm | The place is as present as the person | Action in a place, a follow shot, an arc | Faces near the edge stretch. |
| 35 mm | Person and place both readable | A frozen-moment arc, handheld coverage, a locked wide frame | Close to the generator default. Give it an unusual height or angle. |
| 50 mm | Close to the eye's own perspective | A push-in, a side-on tracking shot | The generator default. Use it only with a decisive position or move. |
| 85 mm | Compressed depth, soft background | A profile close-up, a reaction, a rack focus | Needs distance. Less of the place is visible. |
| 135 mm | Strong compression, the subject isolated | Surveillance, tension, a detail from far away | Needs a lot of distance. The move must be small. |
| 100 mm macro | Extreme close focus, a thin sharp zone | An insert: a grip, a foot, a tool | Hands and text fail in generators. Select another detail. |

## Rules

- Select the lens for a feeling. Write the feeling in the option card: "85 mm, because the face must feel watched".
- Do not use 35 to 50 mm at chest height as the default. If you select 35 or 50 mm, change the height or the angle.
- Use 1 lens for each shot type in a sequence. Example: 18 mm for full bodies, 85 mm for faces.
- Alternate very wide and very long between shots for energy. Do not stay in the middle.
- Keep a wide lens level. Do not tilt it up at a face.
- For a close face, go back and use a long lens. Do not go close with a wide lens.
- A soft background comes from a long lens, a near subject and a far background. Write those 3 facts, not "bokeh".
- A zoom changes the lens during the shot. It has no parallax. A push-in moves the camera and has parallax.
  Write which one you mean.
- In a prompt, write the focal length and the visible effect: "85 mm, the background compressed close and soft".
  A generator follows the described effect more reliably than the number alone.
- A mock image shows a lens feel. It is not a calibrated optical simulation.
