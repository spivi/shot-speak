# Camera placement guide

Read this file when you decide where the camera stands.

State 4 things for every shot: **height, side, distance, tilt**. A missing one becomes the generator default.

## Height

| Height | The viewer feels | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| On the floor | Scale, power, speed | Keep the lens level and look along the ground. Best with 18 to 24 mm. |
| Knee to hip | Energy, a child's or a fighter's world | Good for action and for arcs. |
| The subject's own eye height | Closeness, equality | For a child, go down to the child's eyes. |
| Chest height of a standing adult | Nothing | The default. Use it only with a decisive side, lens or move. |
| High, looking down | The subject is small or weak | Keep it clearly high. A little high looks like a mistake. |
| Overhead, straight down | Pattern, distance | No horizon in the frame. Faces are not readable. |

## Side and angle to the subject

| Position | The viewer feels | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Front | A portrait, an advert | The generator default. Avoid it, except for a decision such as a POV answer shot. |
| Three-quarter front | Natural, readable face | The safe choice for a face. The eyes look past the lens. |
| Profile (side-on) | Tension, shape, purpose | The best position for action, for a stare and for a silhouette. |
| Three-quarter back | The viewer goes with the subject | Shows where the subject looks and goes. |
| Behind | Mystery, arrival | The face is hidden. The place is the subject. |
| Over the shoulder | A relationship between 2 people | A soft shoulder in the foreground gives depth. |

## Distance and shot size

| Shot size | Shows | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Wide | The whole body and the place | Where we are. Scale. Action that needs the whole body. |
| Medium | Waist up | Gestures. Use it with a clear side or height, never as the default. |
| Close-up | Chest or shoulders up | A thought, a reaction. |
| Insert | 1 small detail | Weight and importance. |

## Rules

- **The camera stands where a crew can stand.** A position in the middle of the action or inside an object
  looks unnatural. Go lower and next to the action, or go far away with a long lens, or change the composition.
- **Stay on 1 side of the action line.** Draw a line through the 2 people, or along the direction of travel.
  Keep every camera position on 1 side of it. A subject that travels left to right then keeps that direction
  in every shot.
- **Screen-left is not the character's left.** Write "screen-left" and "screen-right".
- **Eyelines go to a target in the scene.** Name the target. Never the lens.
- **Put the light behind or beside the subject,** then select the camera side that looks toward it.
- **Use depth.** 1 thing near the lens, the subject, 1 thing far away.
- **Frame the part that explains the action:** the hands and the object for a task, the feet and the floor for a landing.
- **For a likeness, 1 person in each shot.** For 2 or more people, give each person a screen side and a clear
  difference in clothing.
- **A camera path must be clear of people and walls.** State the start position and the end position of every move.
